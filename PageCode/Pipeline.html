Our Discovery Pipeline

Leveraging cutting-edge bioinformatics to identify novel antigens for cancer immunotherapy.

Overall Research Pipeline

This research aims to identify potential cancer immunotherapy targets, specifically neoantigens and tumor-associated antigens, by integrating genomic and proteomic data.

1. Sample Collection

The process begins with collecting two sample types from a patient:

  • Normal

    Normal tissue (Peripheral Blood Mononuclear Cells - PBMCs).

  • Tumor

    Tumor tissue from the same patient.

2. Multi-omics Analysis

These samples undergo a comprehensive analysis:

  • Exome Sequencing: Performed on both normal and tumor tissue to find cancer-specific mutations.
  • RNA Sequencing: Conducted on tumor tissue to analyze gene expression.
  • Proteomics: Carried out on tumor tissue to identify the actual proteins present.

3. Antigen Identification

Neoantigens

Derived from tumor-specific mutations identified by comparing exome data from tumor and normal tissues, and confirmed with proteomic evidence.

Tumor-Associated Antigens (TAAs)

Identified by comparing the tumor's RNA expression profile with a normal tissue database to find overexpressed genes.

Detailed Neoantigen Identification Pipeline

The core of our research is this computational pipeline for identifying high-priority neoantigen candidates.

1. Data Preprocessing

Raw sequencing reads (DNA & RNA) are cleaned to ensure quality using Trimmomatic.

2. Alignment to Reference Genome

Cleaned reads are aligned to a human genome. HISAT2 is used for RNA, and BWA for DNA.

3. Removal of PCR Duplicates

Biases from PCR amplification are eliminated using Picard.

4. Somatic Variant Calling & Filtering

Tumor-specific mutations are identified using Samtools, GATK, and SomaticSniper, then germline mutations are excluded.

5. HLA Typing & MHC Binding Prediction

The patient's HLA type is determined, and mutated peptides are analyzed for their binding affinity to the patient's HLA molecules.

6. Ranking, Filtering & Validation

Predicted neoantigens are cross-referenced with proteomics data and ranked. High-priority candidates are then experimentally validated via PCR and sequencing.
