Our Discovery Pipeline

Leveraging cutting-edge bioinformatics and multi-omics data to identify novel antigens for cancer immunotherapy.

Overall Research Pipeline

Our process integrates genomic and proteomic data to identify high-potential neoantigens and tumor-associated antigens for therapeutic development.

1. Sample Collection
  • Normal

    Peripheral Blood Mononuclear Cells (PBMCs).

  • Tumor

    Tumor tissue from the same patient.

    • 2. Multi-omics Analysis
    • Exome Sequencing: Identifies cancer-specific mutations.
    • RNA Sequencing: Analyzes gene expression levels.
    • Proteomics: Confirms protein presence and modifications.

    3. Antigen Identification
    Neoantigens

    Derived from tumor-specific mutations, confirmed with proteomic evidence for high-specificity targets.

    Tumor-Associated Antigens (TAAs)

    Identified by finding overexpressed genes in tumors compared to a normal tissue database.

    Detailed Neoantigen Identification

    Our computational pipeline meticulously filters and ranks candidates to find the most promising neoantigens for therapeutic use.

    1. Data Preprocessing

    Raw DNA & RNA sequencing reads are cleaned and quality-checked using Trimmomatic.

    2. Alignment

    Cleaned reads are aligned to a human genome reference using BWA (DNA) and HISAT2 (RNA).

    3. Variant Calling

    Tumor-specific mutations are identified using GATK, SomaticSniper, and then filtered.

    4. HLA Typing

    Patient's HLA type is determined, and mutated peptides are analyzed for MHC binding affinity.

    5. Ranking & Validation

    Candidates are ranked, cross-referenced with proteomics data, and validated experimentally.
